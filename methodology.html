WHY THIS MODEL EXISTS

Knowing what you actually have, before you ask the world to recognize it

A working assessment methodology for traditional orchards and agricultural landscapes, built to answer one honest question: are we actually ready, and if not, what specifically is missing?

The problem this model answers

A community, a researcher, or a student working on a heritage nomination usually has two bad options: read UNESCO's own dense procedural documents cover to cover, or guess. Neither tells you, plainly, whether a specific piece of evidence is strong enough, or what a real reviewer would actually ask for next.

This model exists to close that gap. It takes UNESCO's own official assessment structure, the Operational Guidelines for the World Heritage Convention and the Operational Directives for the 2003 Intangible Cultural Heritage Convention, and turns it into sixteen concrete stages, each with a real question, a real example, and an honest status: ready, needs evidence, or not yet started.

What the model actually does

Three things, working together.

It routes a site to the right path

World Heritage, ICH Representative, and ICH Urgent Safeguarding are three different conventions with three different bars to clear. The model tracks a site against all three simultaneously, and is honest when the strongest path isn't the one with the highest percentage, a single failed gatekeeper (missing community consent, for example) overrides everything else.

It tells you what's missing, specifically

A real, generated summary: which stages are fully documented, which have never been touched, and, for each unanswered question, what a genuinely strong answer would actually contain, pulled directly from the same guidance UNESCO reviewers themselves use.

It tells the difference between a lead and evidence

Every piece of information in this model carries an honest tier: verified, lead, or genuinely unread. A newspaper article, a photograph, or a documentary can still be exactly the thing that leads to a real breakthrough, so leads are tracked seriously, right alongside verified evidence, just tagged for what they currently are. A question only moves to Ready once real, verified evidence backs it.

Why did we choose these four case studies for Mi'ilya?

Each was reverse-engineered from a real, specific question Mi'ilya's own case raised, then studied from primary sources: the actual nomination dossiers and Committee decisions, to find out what a real reviewer actually asked for.

ICH Representative

Al-Mihrass, Jordan

The closest possible precedent: same region, same subject, ancient olive trees, same path. Studied to learn exactly what a successful file looks like, criterion by criterion.

World Heritage, emergency

Sebastia, Palestine

An active, still-pending emergency nomination. Studied to understand what a threat-driven case looks like, and what real urgency actually requires in a dossier.

ICH Urgent Safeguarding

Turkish olive cultivation

A genuinely different threat framing: production stays viable while the traditional knowledge itself erodes. Studied to learn that distinction precisely.

World Heritage, landscape

Serra de Tramuntana, Spain

The specific precedent behind Mi'ilya's own combined winery-and-orchard question. Read directly from the actual ICOMOS evaluation, revealing a harder, more honest standard than expected.

An AI-assisted research workflow

The model doesn't ask a community to type facts into a form and hope for the best. Real primary sources, nomination dossiers, Committee decisions, academic papers, are read directly, extracted, tagged with an honest confidence tier, and cited. When a written recommendation doesn't exist yet for a path, the model generates one from the real underlying data rather than leaving a blank.

This is the actual answer to a real question worth asking directly: can this model actively read and analyze sources itself? Within a supervised, source-checked workflow, yes, this is what happened across every reference case in this project.

The pages, and what each one is for

This model is a small set of tools, each built for a specific real moment in the work.

The case study page

One place to see a whole site: its history, its evidence, and how it stands across all three UNESCO paths at once. This is where the actual assessment lives, and where the auto-generated summaries and checklists give a real, honest snapshot at a glance.

The path page

A deeper view into one specific path, criterion by criterion. Built for the moment someone wants to focus entirely on, say, the ICH Representative List, without the other two paths taking up space.

The field research packet

Turns "what's missing" into an actual list someone can act on: sorted by priority, printable for the field, or saved directly online with a passcode. Built for the researcher standing in the orchard with real questions to answer.

Interview mode

A generated script for one real conversation. Built around a simple idea: a single interview with the right person can resolve many open questions at once, so the script gathers everything that one conversation could genuinely cover.

Submit evidence

A secure, passcode-protected way to add a real lead directly into the system, reviewed before it's treated as confirmed. Built so anyone can contribute a lead directly, with real database access reserved for the review step.

Add new study case

The model's own growth path, built directly into itself. A name, some general information, and a place to paste anything at all, articles, links, rough notes, becomes a real pending submission, ready to be reviewed and turned into a properly structured case.

The timeline

Real dated facts, shown as an actual chronology instead of sitting buried inside paragraphs. Built directly from a reviewer's own suggestion that a visual timeline would help.

Where this can go

This model can grow well beyond Mi'ilya. The stage structure, the evidence discipline, the auto-generated summaries, and the three-path guidance were built to onboard any orchard or heritage-tree site, anywhere, olive, date palm, fig, pomegranate, grapevine, and walk it through the same rigorous path toward UNESCO readiness. Mi'ilya is the first real case this framework was built against, and a foundation for everything it can hold next.

  • A new site is one row in the database, added without touching the schema.
  • The same sixteen stages, the same evidence-tier discipline, and the same auto-generated guidance apply immediately.
  • A research team studying multiple heritage-tree sites could use this as a shared, standing readiness tracker across every one of them at once.
