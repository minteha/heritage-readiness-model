HERITAGE READINESS MODEL — METHODOLOGY
Version 0.1.0
Paired with: index.html v0.1.0

============================================================
1. WHAT THIS MODEL IS
============================================================

A Heritage Readiness assessment model for traditional orchards and
agricultural landscapes. It does not claim to submit anything to
UNESCO on a site's behalf — official nomination is a state-level
process. Instead, it answers a narrower, more useful question:

  If we wanted to pursue international recognition for this place
  in the future, what do we already have, what is missing, and what
  should be done next?

This is the same function UNESCO's own "Preliminary Assessment"
process serves officially (Operational Guidelines §122) — this model
is a self-serve, accessible version of that same logic, built to be
run by a researcher, community, or local authority before any formal
process begins.

Ma'alia's ancient olive orchards are the first site run through this
model — a pilot case, not the model's sole purpose. The architecture is
built to be reusable: any orchard or agricultural landscape can be
entered as a new site (see the `sites` table in the database).

============================================================
2. THE THREE PATHS
============================================================

"UNESCO recognition" is not one process. This model covers two
conventions and three practical routes:

  World Heritage (1972 Convention)
    - Site- or landscape-based.
    - Satisfy AT LEAST ONE of 10 criteria (an OR-gate).
    - Governed by: Operational Guidelines for the Implementation of
      the World Heritage Convention (WHC.25/01, 16 July 2025).

  Intangible Cultural Heritage — Representative List (2003 Convention)
    - Community- and knowledge-based, not site-based.
    - Satisfy ALL 5 of R.1-R.5 (an AND-gate, no partial credit).
    - Real example: Al-Mihrass tree (Jordan, inscribed 2025).

  Intangible Cultural Heritage — Urgent Safeguarding List (2003 Convention)
    - Same community basis, but the practice must be demonstrably at
      risk of disappearing (U.2 requires this explicitly).
    - Satisfy ALL 6 of U.1-U.6.
    - Real example: Traditional Turkish Olive Cultivation Practices
      (Turkiye, inscribed 2023).
    - Both ICH lists governed by: Operational Directives for the
      Implementation of the 2003 Convention (2024 edition).

A genuinely useful asymmetry to keep in mind: on the World Heritage
side, risk/danger is purely a POST-inscription monitoring category
(the List of World Heritage in Danger) — it never helps a nomination
succeed. On the ICH side, demonstrating urgent risk (U.2) is itself a
PATHWAY to inscription. Same underlying concept, opposite structural
role, depending on which convention applies.

Deliberately excluded, by scope, not oversight: UNESCO Biosphere
Reserves (Man and the Biosphere Programme) and the FAO's GIAHS
(Globally Important Agricultural Heritage Systems) designation. Both
are real, relevant mechanisms for a living agricultural landscape,
but neither follows the individual, criteria-based nomination
structure this model is built around. GIAHS in particular is run by
the FAO, not UNESCO, and is not a "UNESCO announcement" at all.

============================================================
3. THE SIXTEEN STAGES
============================================================

Every question in the model belongs to one of sixteen stages. Eight
map directly onto sections of UNESCO's own official Preliminary
Assessment request format (Operational Guidelines, Annex 3). Eight
go beyond what either convention formally requires at this stage —
marked as extensions because they anticipate what a FULL nomination
will demand later, not because they're less important.

  0  Pathway Selector                        [EXTENSION]
  1  Identification                          [OFFICIAL — Annex 3 §1]
  2  Description                             [OFFICIAL — Annex 3 §2]
  3  Significance & Track Strategy           [OFFICIAL — Annex 3 §3]
  4  Historical Depth & Continuity           [EXTENSION]
  5  Integrity                               [OFFICIAL — Annex 3 §4]
  6  Authenticity                            [OFFICIAL — Annex 3 §5]
  7  Comparative Analysis                    [OFFICIAL — Annex 3 §6]
  8  Community & Intangible Values           [EXTENSION, expands §7.c]
  9  Conservation Status by Component        [EXTENSION, expands §4.b]
  10 Threat Assessment & Risk Matrix         [EXTENSION]
  11 Legal Protection                        [OFFICIAL — Annex 3 §7.a]
  12 Management Plan                         [OFFICIAL — Annex 3 §7.b]
  13 Economic Sustainability                 [EXTENSION]
  14 Boundaries & GIS                        [EXTENSION, expands §1.e]
  15 Dashboard, Gap Analysis & Roadmap       [EXTENSION — the model's
                                              core original contribution]

Design rule carried through the whole schema: "Evidence Required" is
not its own stage. Every question in every stage carries its own
expected evidence type as a built-in property (see the
`assessment_questions.expected_evidence_type_id` field) — this
mirrors how Annex 3 itself works: every official section already
demands specifics and documentation, not a general attachment at
the end.

============================================================
4. THE GATEKEEPER PRINCIPLE
============================================================

The final output (Stage 15) never reduces the assessment to a single
blended score. Status is categorical per stage: Ready / Needs
Evidence / Major Gap / Critical Gap.

Some questions are marked as GATEKEEPERS (`is_gatekeeper = true`).
A failing gatekeeper answer overrides every other answer in its
stage, regardless of how strong the rest of the assessment is. For
example: no free, prior and informed consent (FPIC) on the ICH
track is a Critical Gap on its own — no amount of strong evidence
elsewhere compensates for it. This mirrors how UNESCO itself treats
these criteria: ICH's own AND-gate logic (all of R.1-R.5, or all of
U.1-U.6) already works this way; the model's gatekeeper logic just
makes that structure visible and explicit rather than implicit.

============================================================
5. SOURCE-TYPE TRACEABILITY
============================================================

Every stage, and every question within it, carries a `source_type`
field: either `official_unesco` or `tool_extension`. This is a
database-level field, not just documentation — anyone (a researcher,
a professor, a future collaborator) can query exactly which parts of
this model's logic come directly from UNESCO's own governing texts,
and which parts were added by this project, at any time.

============================================================
6. DATA EXTRACTION METHODOLOGY (for the individual article-processing
   pipeline — DRAFT, for the eventual standalone API function)
============================================================

This section is a working draft of the extraction rules that any
future automated pipeline (e.g. a Supabase Edge Function calling the
Anthropic API directly) must be given explicitly, since a standalone
API call has no memory of the reasoning that produced these rules —
it only knows what is written into its own instructions.

  6.1 Cross-check before insert
      Before adding any new record, check it against what already
      exists in the target table (by name, by citation, or by the
      most specific available identifier). Never assume a new
      source is reporting genuinely new information without this
      check first — the Peqi'in project's extraction history (a
      53-specimen undercount in one chapter, an entire chapter
      missed, two objects given identical basket numbers) is the
      concrete reason this rule exists.

  6.2 Flag ambiguity rather than resolve it silently
      If two sources disagree, or if extracted data does not cleanly
      map onto an existing question or evidence type, flag it for
      human review rather than picking an interpretation
      automatically.

  6.3 Tag source_type on every new question or stage
      Any new question added to `assessment_questions` must be
      explicitly tagged `official_unesco` (traceable to a specific
      paragraph/section of one of the two governing conventions) or
      `tool_extension` (added based on other literature or project
      judgment). Never leave this field to a default.

  6.4 Evidence must be linked, not just asserted
      A claim extracted from an article is not usable on its own —
      it must be written into `evidence_records`, linked to the
      specific `assessment_answers` row it supports, with a real
      source citation.

  6.5 Quantitative thresholds require their source study named
      explicitly (e.g. CBH antiquity thresholds, drought-resilience
      percentages, groundwater-depth triggers) — never presented as
      a general rule without attribution to the specific study it
      came from.

  6.6 Human approval before commit
      No automated extraction writes directly to `assessment_answers`
      or `evidence_records` without an explicit human approval step.
      The pipeline's job is to propose; a person decides.

This draft should be revisited and hardened once several real
articles have been processed manually (see Section 1) and the
extraction rules stop changing significantly between articles —
that stability is the signal that the rules are mature enough to
encode into a standalone, unsupervised function.
