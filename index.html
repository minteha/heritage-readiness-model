HERITAGE READINESS MODEL

Before you nominate, know what you actually have.

A working assessment methodology for traditional orchards and agricultural landscapes. The sixteen stages are based on two key UNESCO documents, which are linked at the bottom of this page.

Pilot case study: the ancient olive orchards of Mi'ilya.

Three paths, not one

"UNESCO recognition" means different things depending on the path. This model covers two conventions and three practical routes, each with a genuinely different bar for what counts as enough.

1972 CONVENTION

World Heritage

Site- or landscape-based. Judged against ten criteria covering culture, history, and land use.

Satisfy at least ONE of 10 criteria
REAL EXAMPLE Sebastia, inscribed via the emergency procedure and placed on the Danger List simultaneously (2026), citing regional conflict.
2003 CONVENTION

ICH: Representative List

Community-centered: the knowledge, skills, and practices themselves are what's protected, separate from any specific place.

Satisfy ALL 5 of R.1–R.5
REAL EXAMPLE Al-Mihrass (Jordan, 2025), the olive-tree listing that this whole project began from.
2003 CONVENTION

ICH: Urgent Safeguarding

Same community basis, but the practice must be demonstrably at risk of disappearing.

Satisfy ALL 6 of U.1–U.6
REAL EXAMPLE Turkish olive cultivation (2023), inscribed because the traditional grafting/milling knowledge was endangered, even as production itself stayed viable.

This model deliberately covers only these three routes. UNESCO Biosphere Reserves (Man and the Biosphere Programme) and the FAO's GIAHS designation are adjacent, genuinely relevant mechanisms, left out by choice, not oversight, because they don't follow this same individual, criteria-based nomination structure.

Before choosing a path: check eligibility per convention, not per organization

UNESCO membership and being a party to one of its specific conventions are two separate things. A state can remain fully active under one convention while not participating in another at all. This needs checking convention by convention, not assumed from a state's general UNESCO status.

Real example: Israel remains a fully active State Party to the 1972 World Heritage Convention, with real inscribed sites and current sessions. It does not currently appear as a party to the 2003 Intangible Cultural Heritage Convention specifically. For a site in Israel, this means the World Heritage path has no eligibility barrier at all, while the two ICH paths would need that separate convention to be ratified first.

Sixteen stages

Every question the model asks belongs to one of sixteen stages. Eight map directly onto UNESCO's own Preliminary Assessment format; eight go beyond it, by deliberate design, each marked here so the distinction is never hidden.

Grounded in the source documents

This page describes the model's stage structure specifically. Evidence gathered for individual sites, like academic papers, interviews, and official records, lives on each site's own page instead. The "Official UNESCO requirement" tags shown throughout this framework trace directly to the two texts below, read in full.

WHC.25/01 · 16 July 2025

Operational Guidelines for the Implementation of the World Heritage Convention

Defines the ten criteria, the three-type Cultural Landscape typology, the seven authenticity attributes, the three integrity conditions, and the official Preliminary Assessment format (Annex 3) this model's stage structure is built on.

2024 edition

Operational Directives for the Implementation of the 2003 Convention

Defines the Representative List (R.1–R.5) and Urgent Safeguarding List (U.1–U.6) criteria, and the free, prior and informed consent (FPIC) standard governing the community-consent stage.
